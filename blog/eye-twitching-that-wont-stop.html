Eye twitching that won't stop: what to note before you see a doctor

A twitching eyelid is common and usually settles on its own. What tends to set it off, what to write down, and when it is worth getting checked.

Symptoms12 October 20265 min readBy the Ocufuse team

A flickering eyelid that you can feel but nobody else seems to notice is one of the most common eye complaints. It usually affects one lower or upper eyelid, comes in bursts, and is far more annoying than it looks. In most people it settles on its own within days or a few weeks.

When it doesn't settle, a short record makes the conversation with your doctor much easier.

Common things that set it off

Eyelid twitching is often linked to everyday factors. Many people notice it more with:

  • Stress or a busy period
  • Short or broken sleep
  • Caffeine, alcohol or nicotine
  • Long stretches of screen or reading work
  • Dry or irritated eyes

Cutting back on one factor at a time, and noting what changes, is more useful than changing everything at once. You won't know what helped otherwise.

What to write down

  • Which eyelid. Left or right, upper or lower.
  • How often and how long. A few seconds now and then, or most of the day.
  • How strong. A faint flicker, or does the eyelid close?
  • What else moved. Only the eyelid, or also the cheek, mouth or other side of the face?
  • The day around it. Sleep, coffee, screen time, stress. A one-line note is enough.

A short video can help, because twitching often stops the moment you sit down in front of a doctor. Film in steady light and hold the phone still at eye height. Our guide to consistent eye photos covers the setup.

When to get it checked

Book an appointment with your GP or eye care professional if:

  • The twitching carries on for more than a few weeks
  • The eyelid closes fully with each twitch, or you have trouble opening the eye
  • Other parts of the face twitch or pull as well
  • The eyelid starts to droop
  • The eye becomes red, swollen or has discharge
Get help straight away iftwitching comes with a suddenly drooping eyelid, sudden double vision, weakness or numbness in the face or body, or difficulty speaking. See the warning signs that need help straight away.
