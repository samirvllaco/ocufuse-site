#

20-20-20 rule
A screen-break habit: every 20 minutes, look about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds. Read the guide →
20/20 vision
Normal sharpness of vision on a letter chart at 20 feet, written 6/6 in metres. Read the guide →

A

Accommodation
The eye's ability to change focus between far and near.
Amblyopia
Reduced vision in one eye that develops in childhood, often called lazy eye.
Astigmatism
An uneven curve of the cornea or lens that can blur vision at any distance. Read the guide →

B

Blind spot
The small part of each eye's field of vision where the optic nerve leaves the eye. Everyone has one. Read the guide →
Blink rate
How often you blink. People tend to blink less while looking at a screen. Read the guide →
Blue light
Light from the sun and from screens. What blue light glasses can and can't do, according to research. Read the guide →
Blurry vision
When things look out of focus, up close, far away or both. Read the guide →
Brock string
A bead-and-string tool some eye care professionals prescribe for practising convergence. Read the guide →

C

Cataract
A clouding of the eye's natural lens, more common with age. Read the guide →
Colour blindness
Seeing some colours differently from most people, also called colour vision deficiency. Read the guide →
Conjunctivitis
Inflammation of the thin layer over the white of the eye, often called pink eye. Read the guide →
Contact lens care
Everyday habits, from handwashing to never sleeping in lenses, that lower the risk of eye infection. Read the guide →
Contrast sensitivity
How well you can tell an object apart from a background of a similar shade.
Convergence
Both eyes turning inward together to look at something close.
Convergence insufficiency
When the eyes find it hard to turn inward together for close work, which can make reading tiring. Read the guide →

D

Diabetic eye screening
Regular checks of the back of the eye for people with diabetes, often before any symptoms. Read the guide →
Dilation
Eye drops that widen the pupil so an eye care professional can see the back of the eye. Read the guide →
Diplopia
The medical word for double vision. See Double vision
Double vision
Seeing two of one object, side by side or one above the other. New double vision needs urgent care. Read the guide →
Drooping eyelid
An upper eyelid that sits lower than usual, also called ptosis. Read the guide →
Dry eyes
Eyes that feel gritty, burning or scratchy, often worse at a screen or in dry air. Read the guide →

E

Exercise log
A simple record of the exercises your eye care professional prescribed. Read the guide →
Eye allergies
Itchy, watery or red eyes as a reaction to pollen, dust, pets or other allergens. Read the guide →
Eye drops
Drops for the eyes, from lubricating drops to prescribed medicine. Read the guide →
Eye exercises
Movements or tasks for the eyes, and what research does and doesn't show about them. Read the guide →
Eye exercise myths
Common claims about eye exercises and what the evidence says. Read the guide →
Eye strain
Tired, aching or uncomfortable eyes after long close work, also called digital eye strain. Read the guide →
Eye test
A check of your vision and eye health by an optometrist or optician, and how often to have one. Read the guide →
Eye twitching
A small, repeated flicker of the eyelid, also called myokymia. Read the guide →

F

Floaters
Small specks or threads that drift across your vision. A sudden shower of new floaters or flashes of light needs urgent care. Read the guide →

G

Glasses prescription
The numbers that describe the lenses you need, such as SPH, CYL, AXIS and ADD. Read the guide →
Glaucoma
A group of conditions that damage the optic nerve, often without early symptoms, which is why regular eye exams matter. Read the guide →

H

Headache
Pain in the head, which people sometimes notice alongside eye strain. Read the guide →
Hyperopia
Long-sightedness: things up close look less clear than things far away. Read the guide →

L

Light sensitivity
Discomfort in bright light, also called photophobia. Read the guide →

M

Myopia
Short-sightedness: things far away look less clear than things up close. Read the guide →

O

Ophthalmologist
A medical doctor who specialises in eyes and eye surgery. Read the guide →
Optician
A technician who fits glasses lenses and frames from a prescription. Read the guide →
Optometrist
An eye care professional who examines eyes, tests vision and prescribes glasses or contact lenses. Read the guide →
Orthoptist
An eye care professional who assesses eye movements and how the two eyes work together.

P

Pencil push-ups
A near-focus exercise where a pencil is moved slowly toward the nose. Read the guide →
Photophobia
The medical word for light sensitivity. See Light sensitivity
Presbyopia
The gradual loss of near focus with age, usually noticed after about 40. Read the guide →
Ptosis
The medical word for a drooping eyelid. See Drooping eyelid
Pupil
The dark opening in the centre of the iris that gets smaller in bright light and larger in dim light. Read the guide →

R

Red eye
Redness in the white of the eye. A red eye with pain or changed vision needs urgent care. Read the guide →
Retina
The light-sensing layer at the back of the eye. Read the guide →

S

Screen time
The hours spent looking at phones, computers and other screens. Read the guide →
Sleep
How long and how well you slept, which many people note next to how their eyes feel. Read the guide →
Strabismus
When the eyes don't point in the same direction, also called a squint.

T

Tear film
The thin layer of tears that covers and protects the front of the eye. Read the guide →
Tired eyes
Eyes that feel heavy, achy or worn out, often late in the day. Read the guide →

U

UV protection
Shielding the eyes from ultraviolet light, for example with sunglasses that block 100% of UV. Read the guide →

V

Visual acuity
How clearly you see detail, usually measured with a letter chart. Read the guide →
