Eye exercise log: tracking what your clinician prescribed

What to record when you do prescribed eye exercises at home, and how to bring it to your review.

Why keep an eye exercise log

An eye exercise log is a dated record of the exercises your clinician prescribed: which ones, how many, and how they felt. It helps your orthoptist or optometrist review progress. A Cochrane review found home eye exercises were done less consistently than supervised ones, so a record makes reviews more accurate.

What to record

FieldExample
Date and timeTuesday, 8:15 am
ExercisePencil push-ups
Amount3 sets of 10, as prescribed
How it feltMild strain on the last set
Anything unusualShort night's sleep, long screen day

Paper, notes app, or Ocufuse

Any format works if you keep it up. A paper sheet or notes app is enough for many people. Ocufuse is useful if you also want dated eye photos and context in the same place:

  • The Practice log records each exercise and the time you did it.
  • Notes about sleep, caffeine, screen time and eye drops sit on the same timeline.
  • The Doctor Brief puts it all on one page for your review appointment.

Ocufuse does not prescribe, adjust or teach exercises, and it does not measure progress. Your clinician does that.

Bringing it to your review

Bring the last two to four weeks, including days you skipped and why. Honest records help your clinician decide whether the plan is working. For the conditions these logs are most used for, read convergence insufficiency explained and pencil push-ups and the Brock string.
