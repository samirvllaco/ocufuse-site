Shade Hunt: find the different color

A free color game for a one-minute screen break. Find the tile that is a slightly different shade before the clock runs out. No download. Free with your email.

Shade Hunt

Loading the game...

Ocufuse games are for fun and screen breaks. They are not a vision test and do not diagnose or treat any condition. See an eye care professional for any concerns.

How to play

  1. Press Start. A grid of tiles appears, all the same colour except one.
  2. Tap the odd tile. On a keyboard, move with the arrow keys and press Enter.
  3. Keep going until the 60 seconds run out. A wrong tap takes 2 seconds off the clock.

Levels and scoring

Your score is the number of odd tiles you find in 60 seconds. The first grid is 2 by 2 with an easy difference. After that the grid grows to 3 by 3, 4 by 4, 5 by 5 and finally 6 by 6, and the difference in lightness shrinks with every find, from 24 percent at the start to about 2.5 percent. Each grid picks a new random colour, so some levels are harder than others. Your best score is saved in this browser.

Tips for a high score

  • Take in the whole grid at once instead of checking tiles one by one. The odd tile often stands out at the edge of your view.
  • Sit straight in front of the screen. On many laptop screens, the viewing angle changes how colours look from top to bottom.
  • Turn off night mode, warm screen filters and this site's eye comfort mode for your best score. They make shades harder to tell apart.
  • When you are stuck, a quick guess costs only 2 seconds, which can be cheaper than staring.
  • Many people see some colours differently, and colour vision deficiency is common. That is normal, and nothing here measures it.

Take a proper screen break

Shade Hunt lasts one minute, so it fits between two tasks. A game is a change of task, but your eyes are still on a screen. After a round, try the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds, and blink fully a few times. Our guide to the 20-20-20 rule explains where it comes from and what a 2022 study found, and screen eye strain: what actually helps covers screen setup, dry air and when to get your eyes checked.

Questions about Shade Hunt

Is Shade Hunt free?

Yes. All three browser games on ocufuse.com are free to play as often as you like, with no ads and no download.

Do I need an account to play?

No account. Just your email to unlock the three free games, once per browser. Your best score is saved in this browser. A free Ocufuse account at app.ocufuse.com keeps your practice history and app game scores on iPhone and web.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It works in any up-to-date browser on a phone, tablet or computer, with touch, a mouse or a keyboard. The game pauses by itself if you switch tabs.

Is this a colour blindness test or a vision test?

No. Shade Hunt is a game for fun and screen breaks. Screen brightness, display settings and room light all change how the tiles look, so a score says nothing about your eyes. If you notice a change in how you see colours, see an eye care professional.

Why do some rounds feel harder than others?

Every grid uses a random colour, and most people pick out small differences in lightness more easily in some colours than in others. Pale yellows and very dark blues are often the hardest.
