How it works

Three small steps a day

  1. Check in

    A 20-second daily log of how your eyes feel, with notes on sleep, screen time or eye drops if you want them.

  2. Take a break

    Play a short game or follow a guided 20-20-20 break between work sessions.

  3. See your journey

    Your streak, a weekly view of your practice and a dated history you can share with your eye doctor.

Daily log

A daily log that takes seconds

Rate how your eyes feel, note screen time and add a photo if you want. Each entry carries the time it happened, so symptoms that come and go are easier to explain later.

  • Your history stays private to your account. It is never sold or used for advertising.
  • Eye photos, export and account deletion are free for everyone.

Your journey

Your habits, over weeks not days

See your streak, your level and a weekly view of what you practised. The Doctor Brief turns your log into one page for your next appointment. During early access your full history and the Doctor Brief are free; later they become part of Ocufuse Plus.

What clinicians see in a Doctor Brief

Eye exercises

Eye exercises for lazy eye, strabismus and screen-heavy days

Many people with a lazy eye (amblyopia), strabismus or convergence insufficiency are given eye exercises or vision therapy to do at home. Ocufuse helps you keep up with them: guided steps, timers, and a practice history you can show your clinician.

  • Clinician exercises unlock once you record that your eye care professional assigned them.
  • Ocufuse doesn't diagnose or treat amblyopia or strabismus, and games don't replace glasses, patching or therapy your eye care professional prescribes.

What eye exercises can and can't do, or read the eye exercise and vision therapy FAQ.

Visual games
Short games that keep your eyes moving and focusing: Zigzag jumps, Smooth pursuit, Letter catch, Sharp sight, Low-light contrast, Track and spot, Steady gaze and Fusion circles.
Exercises from your clinician
Guided companions for the eye exercises orthoptists and optometrists often give for convergence insufficiency, strabismus and lazy eye: Brock string, barrel card, near and far focus, and gaze hold.
Comfort routines
Quick breaks for screen-heavy days: the 20-20-20 break, blink practice, palming, a warm compress and lid massage, and neck and upper back stretches.

Frequently asked questions

Questions people ask

Is Ocufuse free?

Yes, to start. The daily log, eye photos, the first three visual games in the app and the three browser games on this site are free. Ocufuse Plus adds every game, your full history, all 12 guide modules and the Doctor Brief. Plus is coming soon; during early access everything in the web app is free.

Do I need an account to play?

No account. Just your email to unlock the three free browser games, once per browser; your best score is saved in your browser. You need a free account to save your daily log and practice history.

Is this a vision test or a medical app?

No. Ocufuse helps with habits, screen breaks and keeping a record to show your eye doctor. It does not diagnose or treat anything. For any eye concerns, see an eye care professional.

Can I use it on iPhone and on a computer?

Yes. The web app works now in any modern browser, and the iPhone app arrives October 23, 2026. Log in on both with the same account and your data syncs.

When does Ocufuse Plus start?

Plus is coming soon; during early access everything in the web app is free. When it launches you will see the price before anything is charged, and you can cancel anytime from your settings.
