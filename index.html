Eye fitness app for web and iPhone

Train your eyes a few minutes a day, and keep track of it.

Ocufuse is an eye fitness app for web and iPhone. Play short eye training games, follow guided eye exercises, log how your eyes feel, and bring a clear record to your eye doctor. Made for people doing exercises for lazy eye, strabismus or eye strain, and anyone who wants to look after their eye health.

How it works

A few minutes of eye training a day

  1. Play and practise

    Pick eye training games and eye exercises for today, or follow the ones your clinician assigned. Timers and guided steps keep each session short.

  2. Log how your eyes feel

    Note eye strain, double vision, sleep, screen time and eye drops in a tap, with the time it happened. Add eye photos if you want to.

  3. See your progress

    Follow your eye fitness streak and practice history, and share a Doctor Brief of your symptoms and notes with your orthoptist, optometrist or eye doctor.

Eye exercises from your clinician

Eye comfort routines

Quick breaks for screen-heavy days: the 20-20-20 break, blink training, palming, a warm compress and lid massage, face and temple massage, and neck and upper back stretches.

What eye exercises can and can't do

Lazy eye and strabismus

For lazy eye, strabismus and amblyopia exercises

Many people with a lazy eye (amblyopia), strabismus or convergence insufficiency are given eye exercises or vision therapy to do at home. Ocufuse helps you do them regularly and keep a record: guided steps, timers, Brock string and barrel card companions, and a practice history you can show your clinician.

  • Clinician-only exercises unlock once you record that your eye care professional assigned them.
  • Each session ends with a short check-in on how your eyes felt.
  • Ocufuse doesn't diagnose or treat amblyopia or strabismus, and games don't replace glasses, patching or therapy your eye care professional prescribes.

What is Ocufuse?

Ocufuse is an eye fitness and eye health journal for web and iPhone. It has short eye training games, guided eye exercises (including companions for exercises clinicians give for lazy eye, strabismus and convergence insufficiency) and comfort routines for eye strain. You can log how your eyes feel, add optional eye photos, and print or send a Doctor Brief before an appointment. It is not a medical device.

Do eye exercises improve vision?

Eye exercises do not improve nearsightedness, farsightedness, astigmatism or age-related reading problems, according to the American Academy of Ophthalmology. The main exception is convergence insufficiency, where clinician-supervised exercises have good evidence. Glasses, contact lenses or surgery remain the standard ways to correct refractive error.

What is amblyopia?

Amblyopia, often called lazy eye, is reduced vision in one eye caused by abnormal visual development in childhood. The brain favours the other eye. Treatment is most effective in young children and can include glasses, patching the stronger eye, or atropine eye drops, prescribed and monitored by an eye care professional.

What is strabismus?

Strabismus is a misalignment of the eyes, where one eye turns in, out, up or down while the other looks straight ahead. It can be constant or come and go. Treatment depends on the cause and may include glasses, prism lenses, eye muscle surgery, or exercises for some types.

What is vision therapy?

Vision therapy is a structured program of eye exercises and activities supervised by an optometrist or orthoptist to treat certain eye teaming and focusing problems. Sessions usually last 30 to 60 minutes once or twice a week, with exercises to practise at home between visits.

How can Ocufuse help with prescribed eye exercises?

Ocufuse provides a Practice log for exercises your eye care professional prescribes. You record which exercise you did and when, and the log appears in your Doctor Brief for your next review. Ocufuse does not prescribe, adjust or teach exercises, so follow your clinician's instructions.

Is Ocufuse a medical device?

Ocufuse is not a medical device. The app does not diagnose, monitor, treat or prevent any eye condition, and it does not measure eye alignment or label photos as normal or abnormal. It is a personal journal that helps you keep a consistent record to share with a qualified eye care professional.
